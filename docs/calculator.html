    How the numbers work

    Market cap is price × supply. Divide another coin's market cap by the number of PEP that exist, about 104.8 billion, and you get the price one PEP would have at that size. Multiply it by your amount for the value of your PEP.

    Today or at the peak. "Today" uses each coin's current market cap. "At its all-time high" uses the coin's highest price × its supply today. For coins that keep issuing, such as Dogecoin, the real market cap at the peak was lower, because fewer coins existed then.

    Not a forecast. A market cap is what the market pays for a coin today, and nothing here says Pepecoin will reach another coin's size. PEP's supply also grows by 10,000 PEP a block, so the same market cap means a slightly lower price per PEP over time. Supply and inflation.

    Common questions

    How is the value calculated?

    Another coin's market cap divided by Pepecoin's supply gives the price one PEP would have at that size, and that price times your PEP is the value. With the saved snapshot (CoinGecko, October 4–5, 2026): Dogecoin's $14.7 billion market cap ÷ 104.8 billion PEP ≈ $0.14 per PEP.

    Is this a price prediction?

    No. It compares sizes with arithmetic. Prices depend on demand, and nothing here says Pepecoin will reach another coin's market cap.

    Where do the prices come from?

    From CoinGecko's public API: Pepecoin plus the top 100 coins by market cap, loaded in your browser and reused for up to 10 minutes. If they can't be loaded, the page shows a saved snapshot from October 4–5, 2026.
