Pepecoin and Dogecoin

The same rules, ten years apart

Pepecoin is a fork of Dogecoin Core and keeps Dogecoin's monetary rules: the same algorithm, block time, block reward and open-ended supply. What differs is age, size and price.

Both chains add the same 5.26 billion coins a year, so Pepecoin's supply follows Dogecoin's curve about ten years behind it. See the supply and inflation chart.

Same hashrate, very different pay

Hashrate is the computing power mining a chain. Both chains aim for one block every 60 seconds, so hashrate works out to difficulty × 232 ÷ 60 seconds, and equal difficulty means equal hashrate.

HashrateAbout the samePEP 2.9 PH/s, inside DOGE's 2.1–3.4 PH/s
Paid to miners a day1,043× lessPEP $1,302, DOGE $1.36M
Market cap1/1,555of Dogecoin's

How a small coin gets that much hashrate. Merged mining. The Scrypt machines that mine Pepecoin are already paid by Litecoin and Dogecoin, about $1.61M a day in block rewards between them. Pools that add PEP to their merged mining pay it out on top, for the same work and the same electricity. How merged mining works.

What it means for security. Out-mining Pepecoin takes roughly as much Scrypt hardware as out-mining Dogecoin, far more than PEP's own block rewards could pay for.

The catch. The hashrate is there because large pools include PEP in their merged mining. If they dropped it, Pepecoin's hashrate would fall with them. The live pool list is on miningpoolstats.
